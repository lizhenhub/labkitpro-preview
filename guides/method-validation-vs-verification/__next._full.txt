<h1 style="font-size:29px;line-height:1.25;margin:14px 0 6px;">Method validation vs verification: what clause 7.2 actually expects</h1>
<p style="color:#5b6675;font-size:15px;margin:0 0 6px;">A standard method is not self-validating in your lab. Here is the evidence file an assessor asks for.</p>
<p style="color:#8a94a3;font-size:12px;margin:0 0 16px;">Updated 13 November 2026 · 8 min read · Written for working labs and quality teams</p>
<div style="background:#eef4ff;border-left:4px solid #1e4d8b;border-radius:8px;padding:14px 18px;margin:0 0 22px;"><p style="margin:0 0 6px;font-size:11px;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:.6px;color:#1e4d8b;text-transform:uppercase;">Short answer</p><p style="margin:0;font-size:15px;"><b>Verification</b> (7.2.1.3) confirms that your lab — with its own people, instruments, reagents and matrix — can properly perform a published standard method and achieve its stated performance. <b>Validation</b> (7.2.2) establishes, with evidence, that a non-standard, lab-developed or modified method is fit for its intended use, across the parameters relevant to that use. Verification is a confirmation exercise; validation is a demonstration exercise.</p></div>
<div style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;border-radius:8px;padding:12px 16px;margin:0 0 24px;"><p style="margin:0 0 6px;font-size:12px;font-weight:700;color:#5b6675;">On this page</p><ol style="margin:0;padding-left:20px;font-size:13.5px;"><li><a href="#s1" style="color:#2563eb;">Which one applies to your method</a></li><li><a href="#s2" style="color:#2563eb;">The parameters, and what they are for</a></li><li><a href="#s3" style="color:#2563eb;">The readiness file an assessor can trace in ten minutes</a></li><li><a href="#where-it-goes-wrong" style="color:#2563eb;">Where this goes wrong in practice</a></li><li><a href="#faq" style="color:#2563eb;">Frequently asked</a></li></ol></div>
<h2 id="s1" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">Which one applies to your method</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 12px;">The most quoted assessor question is simple: "show me the records that demonstrate you achieved the method's performance with your staff and your equipment, for the samples you actually report." A method SOP and a reagent catalogue are not verification records.</p>
<div style="overflow-x:auto;margin:0 0 16px;"><table style="width:100%;border-collapse:collapse;font-size:13.5px;"><thead><tr><th style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;text-align:left;background:#f3f6fb;">Situation</th><th style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;text-align:left;background:#f3f6fb;">What 7.2 expects</th><th style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;text-align:left;background:#f3f6fb;">Typical evidence</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Published standard method, used exactly as written</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Verification before use</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Precision, bias/trueness or recovery, and the lab's ability to meet the method's stated performance — with records</td></tr><tr><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Standard method used outside its scope, or modified</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Validation of the modification</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">A defined change, plus evidence that performance still meets the intended use</td></tr><tr><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Lab-developed method, or a method from a non-standard source</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Full validation</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Parameter study across the intended matrix, range and concentration levels</td></tr><tr><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Method specified by the customer</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Verification that the lab can run it; clarification if it is unsuitable or outdated</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Contract-review record, plus the performance evidence</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<h2 id="s2" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">The parameters, and what they are for</h2>
<ul style="margin:0 0 14px;padding-left:22px;"><li style="margin-bottom:7px;"><b>Accuracy / trueness</b> — certified reference materials, recovery studies or comparison with a reference method; state the acceptance criterion before you start.</li><li style="margin-bottom:7px;"><b>Precision</b> — repeatability and, where relevant, intermediate precision across days, operators or instruments. A single afternoon's replicates only demonstrate repeatability.</li><li style="margin-bottom:7px;"><b>Specificity / selectivity</b> — can you distinguish the analyte from matrix interferences? This is where a clean-solvent validation fails a real-sample assessment.</li><li style="margin-bottom:7px;"><b>Linearity and range</b> — calibrate across the range you will report, not just the middle of it; document the acceptance criteria and residuals, not only R².</li><li style="margin-bottom:7px;"><b>LOD / LOQ</b> — determined for the matrix, with the approach stated (blank-based, signal-to-noise, or from the calibration).</li><li style="margin-bottom:7px;"><b>Robustness / ruggedness</b> — deliberate small changes (temperature, pH, reagent lot, operator) to show the method tolerates normal variation.</li><li style="margin-bottom:7px;"><b>Measurement uncertainty</b> — the validation data often supply the Type A components of the 7.6 budget, so keep the raw data, not just the summary.</li></ul>
<p style="background:#fff8e8;border:1px solid #f0d9a0;border-radius:8px;padding:11px 15px;margin:0 0 16px;font-size:14px;"><b>Practical note.</b> Validate the <em>method for your scope</em>, not the technique in the abstract. A validation file that demonstrates excellent linearity in pure solvent says nothing about reporting a pesticide in a fatty food matrix at the same limit. Assessors compare the validation range and matrices against your accredited scope — a mismatch is a finding.</p>
<h2 id="s3" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">The readiness file an assessor can trace in ten minutes</h2>
<ol style="margin:0 0 14px;padding-left:22px;"><li style="margin-bottom:9px;">Method version and source, with a documented review that it is the current edition.</li><li style="margin-bottom:9px;">Intended use statement: analytes, matrices, concentration/measurement range, and the customer or regulatory requirement it serves.</li><li style="margin-bottom:9px;">Verification / validation plan with parameters, acceptance criteria and sample sizes defined <em>before</em> the work.</li><li style="margin-bottom:9px;">Raw data, calculations, deviations and the conclusion against each criterion, signed and dated.</li><li style="margin-bottom:9px;">Personnel competence and authorisation for this method (6.2), plus equipment and traceability status (6.4 / 6.5).</li><li style="margin-bottom:9px;">Link to the uncertainty budget (7.6) and to the routine QC / PT programme (7.7) that keeps the method under control.</li><li style="margin-bottom:9px;">Change-control history: any modification triggers a documented re-verification or re-validation.</li></ol>
<h2 id="where-it-goes-wrong" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">Where this goes wrong in practice</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Assuming "it is a standard method" means no work is required. 7.2.1.3 still asks for verification of achievement in your own laboratory.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Validating in a clean solvent while reporting against a complex matrix, or validating a range narrower than the accredited scope.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Setting acceptance criteria after seeing the data, or reporting only R² for linearity without residual analysis.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Treating a minor method change (new instrument model, new reagent lot, shortened incubation) as administrative and never re-verifying.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Keeping the summary report but not the raw data, so the uncertainty budget's Type A components cannot be reconstructed.</p>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">Frequently asked</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 10px;"><b>Does ISO/IEC 17025:2017 require validation of every method?</b><br>No. It requires verification of standard methods before use (7.2.1.3) and validation of non-standard, lab-developed and modified methods (7.2.2). In both cases the extent should be appropriate to the intended use, and records must be retained.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 10px;"><b>What is the difference between verification and confirmation in a testing lab?</b><br>Verification here means confirming your lab can properly operate a specified method and meet its performance. It is not the same as the metrological 'calibration verification' of an instrument, nor the customer's 'verification' of a product. Keep the terms distinct in your procedure.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 10px;"><b>How many replicates are needed for a validation study?</b><br>There is no universal number. The plan should be justified by the intended use, the variability and the acceptance criteria — commonly multiple days, operators and concentration levels so that intermediate precision can be estimated. Sector guidance or the method itself may prescribe a design.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 10px;"><b>When must a method be re-validated?</b><br>When it is modified, extended to a new matrix or range, moved to substantially different equipment, or when QC / PT data indicate performance has changed. Document the trigger in your change-control procedure rather than relying on a fixed calendar.</p>
