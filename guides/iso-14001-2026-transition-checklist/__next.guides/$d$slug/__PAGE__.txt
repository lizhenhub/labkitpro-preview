<h1 style="font-size:29px;line-height:1.25;margin:14px 0 6px;">The ISO 14001:2026 transition: the dates, the eleven changes, the three records</h1>
<p style="color:#5b6675;font-size:15px;margin:0 0 6px;">Published 15 April 2026, cutoff 30 April 2029 — the earliest of the three systems. Here is the work, in the order an auditor will check it.</p>
<p style="color:#8a94a3;font-size:12px;margin:0 0 16px;">Updated 13 November 2026 · 9 min read · Written for working labs and quality teams</p>
<div style="background:#eef4ff;border-left:4px solid #1e4d8b;border-radius:8px;padding:14px 18px;margin:0 0 22px;"><p style="margin:0 0 6px;font-size:11px;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:.6px;color:#1e4d8b;text-transform:uppercase;">Short answer</p><p style="margin:0;font-size:15px;"><b>This is not a manual rewrite.</b> ISO 14001:2026 (published 15 April 2026) changes eleven things that fall into four workstreams: broader environmental conditions in 4.1 and 4.2 and a life-cycle argument in 4.3; a new standalone change-management clause 6.3 and a restructured 6.1; a hard link from 6.1.2 to 8.2 for emergency scenarios; and site-wide renumbering and terminology changes. The cutoff is <b>30 April 2029</b>, but your certification body cannot issue a 2026-edition certificate until its accreditation body completes the transition decision, due 30 April 2027.</p></div>

<p style="background:#fff8e8;border:1px solid #f0d9a0;border-radius:8px;padding:11px 15px;margin:0 0 16px;font-size:14px;"><b>Practical note.</b> Book the transition audit into your next surveillance visit and get written confirmation that your CB has passed its AB transition decision. At least one witnessed assessment of your CB must be conducted against the 2026 edition — your transition visit may well be that witnessed audit, so do not plan on a formality.</p>
<h2 id="s2" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">The eleven changes, grouped into four workstreams</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 12px;">Annex A is informative, not auditable on its own, and clarifies three themes: how context, risks and opportunities and other clauses interrelate; the life-cycle perspective; and alignment with strategy, business processes and integrated management systems. It still decides the vocabulary your auditor uses, so it belongs in internal-auditor retraining even though nobody should be collecting evidence against it clause by clause.</p>

<h2 id="s3" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">The three records an auditor will sample first</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 12px;">Then they open a procedure at random and read the clause references. If old 10.1 citations survive — or old 10.3 citations were never changed to 10.1 — the revision only changed the cover page.</p>
<ul style="margin:0 0 14px;padding-left:22px;"><li style="margin-bottom:7px;"><b>The 6.1.2 emergency-scenario list versus the 8.2 plan.</b> 6.1.2 now states that the organisation shall determine potential emergency situations, with an explicit reference to 8.2. If the scenario list lives only inside the emergency plan and cannot be read from the aspects evaluation records, that mismatch is the single most common finding.</li><li style="margin-bottom:7px;"><b>One real change, end to end.</b> For clause 6.3, an auditor picks a genuine change and walks its review, approval, implementation and verification records. A process that no real change has completed in six months looks like a paper process; back-filled records with same-date signatures or jumping version numbers are easy to spot.</li><li style="margin-bottom:7px;"><b>The latest internal audit.</b> Under 9.2.2 a) every audit must define its objectives, criteria and scope. The auditor checks whether the objective exists, then whether the report is structured around it, then how the audit programme's own effectiveness was evaluated.</li></ul>
<h2 id="where-it-goes-wrong" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">Where this goes wrong in practice</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Writing one word, applicable, against all five environmental-condition dimensions in 4.1 instead of a signed relevant-or-not-relevant call with a reason that traces to 6.1 risks and opportunities.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Keeping the emergency-scenario list only in the 8.2 plan, so the 6.1.2 evaluation records show no evidence that potential emergencies were determined.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Treating the informative Annex A as a checklist of requirements to collect evidence against, or the opposite mistake of never retraining internal auditors on its three themes.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Waiting until 2029 to schedule the transition audit, without checking that the certification body has passed its own AB transition decision first.</p>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">Frequently asked</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 10px;"><b>Is our 2015-edition certificate still valid until 30 April 2029?</b><br>Yes, existing certificates remain valid during the transition period. Two dates bite earlier, though: from 2027-10-31 new initial certifications are issued against the 2026 edition only, and your CB may not issue 2026-edition certificates until its AB transition decision is complete, due 2027-04-30. Confirm the actual schedule in writing with your own certification body.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 10px;"><b>Do we have to rewrite our environmental manual?</b><br>No. ISO 14001 does not require a document called a manual, and the 2026 edition does not add one. Revise the controlled documents and records the changes actually touch — the context analysis, aspects and risk register, emergency plan, supplier list, audit programme, management-review agenda and every clause citation.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 10px;"><b>Is Annex A auditable?</b><br>Annex A is informative guidance, so an auditor should not raise a nonconformity against it directly. But it explains how the mandatory clauses are to be read — A.4.1 on interrelated environmental conditions, A.6.3 on change sources, A.8.2 on the a) to j) emergency considerations — so your documents, training and internal audits should answer in that same language.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 10px;"><b>How does this interact with ISO 9001:2026 and the expected ISO 45001 revision?</b><br>The 14001 cutoff of 2029-04-30 is the earliest; the ISO 9001:2026 cutoff is 2029-09-30. Integrated transition is the officially supported route, which is why the 14001 surveillance visit is usually the first domino. The ISO 45001 revision is still at DIS 40.60, expected in the first half of 2027 with transition arrangements not yet published — keep working to the current 2018 edition and do not rewrite files against an unpublished text.</p>
