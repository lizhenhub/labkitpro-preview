<h1 style="font-size:29px;line-height:1.25;margin:14px 0 6px;">AIAG-VDA FMEA: why Action Priority replaced the RPN (and what to do differently)</h1>
<p style="color:#5b6675;font-size:15px;margin:0 0 6px;">The same RPN can hide two completely different risks. The AP table is a lookup, not a multiplication.</p>
<p style="color:#8a94a3;font-size:12px;margin:0 0 16px;">Updated 13 November 2026 · 7 min read · Written for working labs and quality teams</p>
<div style="background:#eef4ff;border-left:4px solid #1e4d8b;border-radius:8px;padding:14px 18px;margin:0 0 22px;"><p style="margin:0 0 6px;font-size:11px;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:.6px;color:#1e4d8b;text-transform:uppercase;">Short answer</p><p style="margin:0;font-size:15px;">In the AIAG-VDA 2019 FMEA handbook, Risk Priority Number (S × O × D) was replaced by <b>Action Priority (AP)</b>: H, M or L is determined by looking up the combination of Severity, Occurrence and Detection in the official table. It is not a product and not a weighted score. The change exists because a high-severity failure with moderate occurrence and good detection can score the same RPN as a trivial but poorly detected one — while deserving completely different action.</p></div>
<div style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;border-radius:8px;padding:12px 16px;margin:0 0 24px;"><p style="margin:0 0 6px;font-size:12px;font-weight:700;color:#5b6675;">On this page</p><ol style="margin:0;padding-left:20px;font-size:13.5px;"><li><a href="#s1" style="color:#2563eb;">The worked example that killed RPN</a></li><li><a href="#s2" style="color:#2563eb;">How to apply AP correctly</a></li><li><a href="#s3" style="color:#2563eb;">Where AP fits in the seven-step method</a></li><li><a href="#where-it-goes-wrong" style="color:#2563eb;">Where this goes wrong in practice</a></li><li><a href="#faq" style="color:#2563eb;">Frequently asked</a></li></ol></div>
<h2 id="s1" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">The worked example that killed RPN</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 12px;">Both rows score 120 under the old method, so an RPN threshold rule ("act on everything above 100") would treat them identically. Look up S/O/D in the AIAG-VDA AP table instead: (10, 4, 3) lands in the high-severity band and returns H, while (3, 4, 10) lands in the low-severity band and returns L — the same RPN, two completely different actions. That is the whole reason the multiplication was dropped.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 12px;">A second flaw in RPN: it is not an ordinal scale. Gaps between products are uneven, many different S/O/D triples produce the same number, and a team can "improve" an RPN by shaving a point off Detection without reducing any real risk.</p>
<div style="overflow-x:auto;margin:0 0 16px;"><table style="width:100%;border-collapse:collapse;font-size:13.5px;"><thead><tr><th style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;text-align:left;background:#f3f6fb;">Failure mode</th><th style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;text-align:left;background:#f3f6fb;">S</th><th style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;text-align:left;background:#f3f6fb;">O</th><th style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;text-align:left;background:#f3f6fb;">D</th><th style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;text-align:left;background:#f3f6fb;">RPN = S×O×D</th><th style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;text-align:left;background:#f3f6fb;">AP from the official table</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Loss of braking function (safety, special characteristic)</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">10</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">4</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">3</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">120</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">H — action needed (or a documented justification)</td></tr><tr><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">Cosmetic scuff on a non-visible surface</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">3</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">4</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">10</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">120</td><td style="border:1px solid #e6e9ef;padding:8px 10px;vertical-align:top;">L — monitoring may be enough</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<h2 id="s2" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">How to apply AP correctly</h2>
<ol style="margin:0 0 14px;padding-left:22px;"><li style="margin-bottom:9px;">Score S from the <b>effect</b> of the failure, on the team's agreed 1–10 scale. Severity belongs to the function and its consequence, not to how good your inspection is.</li><li style="margin-bottom:9px;">Score O from the occurrence of the <b>failure cause</b> with the current prevention controls in place; score D from the current detection controls' ability to find the cause or failure mode before it escapes.</li><li style="margin-bottom:9px;">Look up S/O/D in the official AP table to get H / M / L. Do not average, multiply or invent your own weighting — the table is the method.</li><li style="margin-bottom:9px;">For H, take action or document why no action is possible; for M, actions should be considered; for L, monitoring may be enough. Some organisations and customers require action on every H — check the customer-specific requirements.</li><li style="margin-bottom:9px;">Record the recommended action, owner, due date, evidence of completion and the re-scored S/O/D, then re-derive AP. An FMEA with no re-scored column is a snapshot, not a living document.</li></ol>
<p style="background:#fff8e8;border:1px solid #f0d9a0;border-radius:8px;padding:11px 15px;margin:0 0 16px;font-size:14px;"><b>Practical note.</b> Severity can only come down when the design or process function changes so the effect is less serious. Adding a camera, a sensor or a second inspection reduces Detection, not Severity. If a team reports a lower S after adding inspection, the scoring is wrong.</p>
<h2 id="s3" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">Where AP fits in the seven-step method</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 12px;">The AIAG-VDA method runs: planning &amp; preparation → structure analysis → function analysis → failure analysis (failure chain of effect / mode / cause) → risk analysis (S, O, D, AP) → optimisation (actions and re-assessment) → results documentation. AP sits in risk analysis and drives optimisation; it is not a final grade for the part.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 12px;">Product and process FMEAs are linked: special characteristics identified in the DFMEA must flow into the PFMEA and then into the control plan, with the corresponding control method and reaction plan. When a customer audit finds a "special characteristic" that exists in the FMEA but not on the control plan, the AP discussion is moot — the loop was never closed. IATF 16949 is published by the International Automotive Task Force, not by ISO, and its customer-specific requirements govern which FMEA reference edition applies.</p>
<h2 id="where-it-goes-wrong" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">Where this goes wrong in practice</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Computing both AP and RPN and sorting by RPN anyway. This reintroduces exactly the problem AP was designed to remove.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Reducing Severity by improving detection controls, or re-scoring S to make an H row become M.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Filling the action column with "increase inspection" for every H row. For high-severity failure modes, prevention (design or process change) is the meaningful action.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Leaving the special-characteristics chain unclosed between DFMEA, PFMEA and control plan, or never re-scoring after an action is completed.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 11px;">Quoting the AP table from memory. Use the published table in the handbook your customer requires; the H/M/L boundaries are specific combinations, not a formula.</p>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-size:20px;margin-top:30px;margin-bottom:8px;">Frequently asked</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 10px;"><b>Is RPN still allowed under AIAG-VDA 2019?</b><br>The harmonised AIAG-VDA method uses Action Priority rather than RPN. Some customers, legacy internal procedures and older SAE J1739-based systems still ask for RPN — follow the customer-specific requirement and do not mix the two scoring systems in one analysis.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 10px;"><b>What do H, M and L actually mean?</b><br>They are action priorities, not risk rankings. H means action is needed (or a documented justification for no action), M means action should be considered, L means action could be taken or the risk may be accepted. The exact wording and any mandatory-action rule come from your customer-specific requirements.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 10px;"><b>Can a low-AP item still need action?</b><br>Yes. Very high severity, regulatory requirements, special characteristics and customer-specific requirements can mandate action regardless of the AP letter. AP is a prioritisation tool inside the FMEA, not a substitute for those obligations.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 10px;"><b>Does ISO 9001:2026 require FMEA?</b><br>No. FMEA is not an ISO 9001 requirement; it is expected through IATF 16949 and automotive customer-specific requirements, and used voluntarily elsewhere as a risk-based-thinking tool.</p>
